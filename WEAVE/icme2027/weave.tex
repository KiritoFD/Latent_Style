\begin{abstract}
Lightweight style transfer is built for consumer devices, yet compact models routinely return
images no more stylized than an unchanged copy of the input, and standard content metrics reward this
identity shortcut. Two zero-cost references, the source and a reference image of the target style,
bracket valid transfer and expose it: eight of twelve baselines leave the bracket on at least one of
three benchmarks. The cause is spectral. In latent velocity matching, coarse structure carries most of
the gradient energy yet is the least style-discriminative band. WEAVE removes this imbalance in the
Haar subbands of a frozen autoencoder latent: a source-anchored target provably suppresses structural
motion, a compact network learns band-weighted texture transport, and stepwise statistical alignment
injects high-frequency style. Trained in 1.4 minutes on one consumer GPU with 1.04M parameters, WEAVE
is valid on every benchmark and Pareto-optimal against all baselines at 512 pixels.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Style transfer now runs in camera apps, creative tools, and on-device media pipelines, where memory
and latency are tight. The strongest results come from diffusion editors and image foundation models
that run a billion-parameter denoiser per image, some needing more than 60\,GB of GPU
memory~\cite{wu2025qwenimagetechnicalreport,blackforestlabs2025flux2}. Compact models fit the
budget~\cite{wang2023microast,liu2024samst,liu2024samam}, but do they actually change the style? We
show that they often do not, and we build one that does.

\noindent\textbf{The identity shortcut.} Simply copying the input scores a \clips{} of 0.693 on our
main benchmark, beating two recent compact models (SaMam 0.582, SaMST 0.618). ArtFID~\cite{wright2022artfid}
ranks the copy first (Fig.~\ref{fig:page1}c), and LPIPS~\cite{zhang2018unreasonable} declares it perfect.
We therefore report two zero-cost outputs with every metric: IDT returns the source, and TGT returns a
reference image of the target style. A valid transfer must beat IDT on style and stay closer to the
source than TGT on content. Under this IDT--TGT sandwich, eight of twelve baselines fail on at least one
benchmark, and every compact baseline (under 10M trainable parameters) is, somewhere, no more stylized
than a copy (Fig.~\ref{fig:page1}b).

\noindent\textbf{Why compact models take the shortcut.} The cause is the training signal, not capacity.
Style transfer must keep structure while changing texture, yet a velocity field regressed on the whole
latent scores both with one loss. In Haar coordinates, the coarse LL band carries 69.5\% of the gradient
energy across 5{,}000 cross-style pairs while being the least style-discriminative band: its
between-/within-style variance ratio is 0.12, against 0.56 for the finest band HH (Fig.~\ref{fig:spectral}).
This imbalance is a property of the regression target, so widening the network leaves it intact.
Networks that fit low frequencies first~\cite{rahaman2019spectral,xu2022frequency} then settle near the
source, while large editors escape only by overwriting the image.

\noindent\textbf{WEAVE} resolves the conflict by giving structure and style their own subbands of a frozen
SD1.5 VAE latent~\cite{rombach2022high}. A source-anchored endpoint restricts the requested LL motion to a
channel-wise affine change of the source, which provably removes at least 91\% of its energy and cuts
LL's share from 69.5\% to at most 17\%. A 1.04M-parameter velocity network learns band-weighted transport,
steered by pooled, coordinate-free codes of the reference, and an AdaIN update aligns high-frequency
statistics after every Euler step. Training takes 82.8\,s on one RTX~3060 and stops itself at an internal
gradient transition. WEAVE is the only compact model valid on all three benchmarks, is Pareto-optimal
against all twelve baselines at 512\,px, and is 11$\times$ smaller and at least 11$\times$ faster than
every other valid learned model.

\noindent\textbf{Contributions.}
(1)~\emph{Evaluation:} the IDT--TGT sandwich, a zero-cost, metric-agnostic validity test that exposes the
identity shortcut and content collapse, and an audit of twelve methods on three benchmarks.
(2)~\emph{Analysis:} a subband measurement and a bound that tie the shortcut to low-frequency gradient
dominance and dictate the transport target.
(3)~\emph{Method:} wavelet-decoupled velocity matching with orientation-specific texture conditioning,
stepwise high-frequency alignment, and metric-free stopping.
(4)~\emph{Results:} the only compact model valid everywhere, Pareto-optimal at 512\,px, with 1.04M
parameters and 1.4 training minutes, plus a training-free plug-in that improves a frozen SD1.5 editor.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\noindent\textbf{Arbitrary and lightweight style transfer.} Gatys et al.\ optimized Gram
statistics~\cite{gatys2015neural,gatys2016image}; AdaIN and WCT made transfer
feed-forward~\cite{huang2017arbitrary,li2017universal}. SANet, StyTr$^2$, and AesPA-Net add
attention~\cite{park2019sanet,deng2022stytr2,hong2023aespa}, ArtFlow avoids content leak with reversible
flows~\cite{an2021artflow}, and CUT learns unpaired translation by patch contrast~\cite{park2020cut}.
MicroAST uses micro encoders and a micro decoder~\cite{wang2023microast}, and Mamba-based SaMST and SaMam
reduce sequence cost~\cite{liu2024samst,liu2024samam,gu2023mamba}. Frequency cues appear in FDD, which
mixes content and style spectra~\cite{li2023fdd}, in AesFA, which decomposes features by
frequency~\cite{kwon2024aesfa}, and in the wavelet-corrected WCT$^2$~\cite{yoo2019photorealistic}. These
methods separate frequencies inside a feed-forward decoder; WEAVE makes Haar subbands the coordinates of
the transport objective itself, with per-band targets, weights, and updates dictated by a measured imbalance.

\noindent\textbf{Diffusion editors and fast translation.} Latent diffusion~\cite{rombach2022high} enables
SDEdit, Prompt-to-Prompt, StyleID, StyleAligned, InST, Z-STAR, IP-Adapter, and
StyleShot~\cite{meng2021sdedit,hertz2022prompt,chung2024style,hertz2024style,zhang2023inst,deng2024zstar,ye2023ipadapter,gao2024styleshot}.
SD-Turbo distills sampling to one step~\cite{sauer2023adversarial}, and LBM performs one-step latent
bridge matching~\cite{chadebec2025lbm}. All of them run a large backbone for every image; WEAVE keeps only the
frozen VAE, and its Haar operator even improves such an editor without retraining.

\noindent\textbf{Flow matching and frequency-aware generation.} Rectified flow and flow
matching~\cite{liu2022rectified,lipman2022flow} regress velocities along endpoint interpolations, and
Schr\"odinger bridges~\cite{debortoli2021dsb,shi2023dsbm,liu2023i2sb,su2023ddib} handle unpaired domains.
FDIT constrains frequency bands separately for translation~\cite{cai2021fdit}, FreqFlow reweights
frequencies for class-conditional generation~\cite{ren2026freqflow}, and wavelet diffusion accelerates
sampling~\cite{phung2023wavelet}. WEAVE changes the endpoint, basis, and band weights of the regression itself.

\noindent\textbf{Evaluation.} LPIPS~\cite{zhang2018unreasonable} and ArtFID~\cite{wright2022artfid} are
standard, yet neither penalizes a copy, and a recent review finds no consensus on style-transfer
evaluation~\cite{ioannou2024evaluation}. The sandwich gives every metric a direction at zero cost.

\section{Diagnosing the Identity Shortcut}
\label{sec:diagnosis}

\subsection{The IDT--TGT Sandwich}
A request consists of a source image $x$ and a target style $s$, represented by a reference image $r_s$.
We set $y_{\mathrm{IDT}}=x$ and $y_{\mathrm{TGT}}=r_s$ and call a method \emph{valid} on a benchmark if
its benchmark means satisfy
\begin{equation}
S(y)>S(y_{\mathrm{IDT}})\ \ \forall S\in\mathcal S,\qquad
C(y)\succ C(y_{\mathrm{TGT}})\ \ \forall C\in\mathcal C,
\label{eq:sandwich}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal S=\{\text{\dinos},\text{\clips}\}$, $\mathcal C=\{\text{LPIPS},\text{\dinoc}\}$, and $\succ$
means closer to the source. The first condition rules out the identity shortcut, the second rules out
content collapse. The test costs nothing, works in raw metric units, needs no weights or thresholds, and
applies to any style and content metric. Copying is a deceptively strong baseline because content images
already share features with any art collection: on D5, IDT scores \dinos{}~0.419 and \clips{}~0.693.

\subsection{Spectral Dominance in Latent Velocity Matching}
Let $\mathcal W(z)=(\ell,h_1,h_2,h_3)$ be the one-level orthonormal Haar transform of a latent
$z\in\mathbb R^{4\times H\times W}$, with LL band $\ell$ and the oriented bands LH, HL, and HH. Flow
matching~\cite{lipman2022flow,liu2022rectified} regresses a velocity $v$ onto the displacement $u=z_1-z_0$
between endpoints. By Parseval, $\|v-u\|^2=\sum_b\|v_b-u_b\|^2$: the loss splits into independent band terms,
and at initialization the share of band $b$ in the gradient energy equals its share of the displacement
energy, $\|u_b\|^2/\|u\|^2$. Fig.~\ref{fig:spectral} measures both on 5{,}000 cross-style D5 pairs with the
direct target $z_1=z_s$. LL holds 69.5\% of the gradient energy, yet its style separability, the
between-/within-style variance ratio of per-image band statistics, is the lowest (0.12 versus 0.56 for HH).
The objective is driven by the band that matters least for style, and moving $\ell_c$ toward $\ell_s$ asks
the network to reproduce the unrelated layout of $x_s$, which it can neither predict nor should copy.


\section{WEAVE}
\label{sec:method}


\subsection{Setting}
WEAVE is trained on unpaired image collections of $K$ styles. A request names a target style by its index
$s$ and one reference image $x_s$; at test time this is the first held-out image of the style, the same
image used for TGT. We work on latents $z_c=\mathcal E(x_c)$ and $z_s=\mathcal E(x_s)$ of the frozen VAE,
cached once before training, and write $P_Lz=\mathcal W^{-1}(\ell,0,0,0)$ and $P_H=I-P_L$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:framework}).

\subsection{Source-Anchored Endpoint}
WEAVE anchors structure at the source and takes texture from the reference:
\begin{equation}
z_1^\star=\mathcal W^{-1}\big((1{-}\alpha)\ell_c+\alpha\,\mathrm{AdaIN}(\ell_c,\ell_s),\;h_{1,s},h_{2,s},h_{3,s}\big),
\label{eq:target}
\end{equation}
with $\alpha=0.3$ and channel-wise spatial statistics in AdaIN~\cite{huang2017arbitrary}.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:anchor}
For one channel, let $\ell_c,\ell_s\in\mathbb R^N$ have means $\mu_c,\mu_s$, standard deviations
$\sigma_c,\sigma_s$, and correlation $\rho$. The LL velocity requested by \eqref{eq:target},
$u_\ell=\alpha(\mathrm{AdaIN}(\ell_c,\ell_s)-\ell_c)$, is an affine function of $\ell_c$ and
\begin{equation}
\|u_\ell\|^2=\alpha^2N\big[(\sigma_s-\sigma_c)^2+(\mu_s-\mu_c)^2\big]\le\alpha^2\|\ell_s-\ell_c\|^2.
\label{eq:bound}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{IEEEproof}
$\mathrm{AdaIN}(\ell_c,\ell_s)-\ell_c=(\sigma_s/\sigma_c-1)(\ell_c-\mu_c)+(\mu_s-\mu_c)$ gives the equality.
Since $\|\ell_s-\ell_c\|^2=N[\sigma_s^2+\sigma_c^2-2\rho\sigma_s\sigma_c+(\mu_s-\mu_c)^2]$, the gap is
$2N\sigma_s\sigma_c(1-\rho)\ge0$.
\end{IEEEproof}

Two consequences follow. The requested structural motion only changes per-channel gain and offset,
i.e., palette and contrast, and contains no layout of $x_s$. Because the high-frequency target is
unchanged, averaging \eqref{eq:bound} over the pairs of Fig.~\ref{fig:spectral} lowers LL's share of
the displacement energy from 69.5\% to at most 17.0\%. Training samples
$z_t=(1-t)z_c+tz_1^\star-\sigma\sqrt{t(1-t)}\,\epsilon$ with $t\sim\mathcal U[0,1]$,
$\epsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,I)$, and $\sigma=0.02$; the label is the clean displacement $u=z_1^\star-z_c$,
and the perturbation, which vanishes at both endpoints, is absent at inference.

\subsection{Band-Weighted Velocity Matching}
The network $v_\theta(z_t,t,s,x_s)$ has a shared trunk of four width-64 residual blocks and separate
$1{\times}1$ heads for LL, LH, and HL. With $(u_\ell,u_{h_1},u_{h_2},u_{h_3})=\mathcal W(u)$, it minimizes
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L=\mathbb E_{t}\big[\lambda_{LL}\|v_\ell-u_\ell\|^2+\|v_{h_1}-u_{h_1}\|^2+\|v_{h_2}-u_{h_2}\|^2\big],
\label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
with $\lambda_{LL}=0.3$. The endpoint \eqref{eq:target} changes the \emph{requested} LL velocity, whereas
$\lambda_{LL}$ changes its optimization pressure. A style memory indexed by $s$ gives the trunk a coarse
prior. For image-specific texture, the reference LH and HL bands are pooled into coordinate-free,
orientation-specific codes that modulate only the matching heads; feeding raw spatial reference bands
instead leaks the reference layout (\dinoc{} falls to 0.40 in our probes; see supplement). HH has no
learned head. The model has 1{,}037{,}087 trainable parameters.

\subsection{Stepwise High-Frequency Alignment}
Velocity matching organizes the motion but leaves a weak appearance signal. With $q=P_Hz$, $q_s=P_Hz_s$,
and $\mathcal A(q;q_s)=\sigma(q_s)(q-\mu(q))/\sigma(q)+\mu(q_s)$ over channel-wise spatial statistics, each
of the $K$ inference steps is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\bar z^{k+1}&=z^k+\tfrac1K\,\mathcal W^{-1}\big(v_\ell^k,v_{h_1}^k,v_{h_2}^k,0\big),\\
z^{k+1}&=P_L\bar z^{k+1}+(1-\beta)\,\bar q+\beta\,\mathcal A\big(\bar q;(1+\eta)q_s\big),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:step}
\end{equation}
where $\bar q=P_H\bar z^{k+1}$, $K=8$, $\beta=2$, and $\eta=0.1$. With $\beta>1$ the update over-relaxes
toward the matched statistics; applied at every step, alignment becomes part of the trajectory rather than
a post-process and supplies HH statistics without a learned head.

\subsection{Metric-Free Stopping}
Training needs no decoding, inversion, or discriminator and runs 52 steps per epoch at batch 96. After
each epoch, a probe batch of four fixed latents at $t=0.5$ gives $\rho_e$, the ratio of LL-loss to HF-loss
gradient norms on the shared trunk. Training stops at the first epoch in which the gate of the texture
route grows and $\rho_e/\rho_{e-1}\le0.65$, i.e., when high-frequency learning takes over the trunk. On D5
the rule fires at epoch~4 ($\rho$ drops from 1.09 to 0.16) after 82.8\,s, without any image metric.

\subsection{Setup}
\noindent\textbf{Benchmarks.} \emph{D5-512}: five WikiArt styles (Early Renaissance, Impressionism,
Minimalism, Rococo, Ukiyo-e) at 512\,px with 3{,}600 training images each; 30 held-out sources per style are
sent to all five styles (750 requests, same-style requests kept). \emph{P2A-256}: the same layout over photo,
Monet, Van Gogh, C\'ezanne, and Hayao at 256\,px. \emph{R5-WikiArt}: five randomly drawn WikiArt families
(Cubism, Expressionism, Pop Art, Romanticism, Symbolism) at 512\,px.

\noindent\textbf{Metrics.} \dinos{} (primary) is the maximum cosine similarity between the DINOv2-small CLS
feature~\cite{oquab2023dinov2} of the output and those of up to 30 held-out references of the target style;
it rewards resembling some member of the family rather than its average. \clips{} is the CLIP
ViT-B/32~\cite{radford2021clip} similarity to the target-style prototype, \dinoc{} the DINOv2 similarity to
the source, and LPIPS uses AlexNet.

\noindent\textbf{Baselines and implementation.} We compare training-free diffusion editors
(SD-Turbo~\cite{sauer2023adversarial}, StyleAligned, Z-STAR, StyleShot, StyleID), learned models (CUT, SaMST,
SaMam, StyTr$^2$, AesPA-Net), the Seedream~4.5 API~\cite{seedream2025}, and an analytic Latent-WCT, each at its
released or recommended operating point. WEAVE uses AdamW (learning rate $2\times10^{-4}$, cosine decay),
bf16, and one RTX~3060 (12\,GB). Training time excludes one-off latent caching; inference time includes
eight ODE steps and VAE decoding.


\subsection{Main Results}
\noindent\textbf{The audit.} Eight of twelve baselines fail the sandwich on at least one benchmark
(Table~\ref{tab:main}, Fig.~\ref{fig:page1}b). SD-Turbo's best-in-class D5 LPIPS (0.003) is a near-perfect
copy: its \clips{} equals that of IDT. SaMam, the strongest compact baseline on content, falls below IDT in
\clips{} on two benchmarks, so its low LPIPS comes at the price of style. StyleAligned tops \dinos{}
everywhere but drifts farther from the source than the reference image itself on D5 and R5. Latent-WCT
never clears IDT: wavelet statistics alone do not transfer style; the learned transport does.

\noindent\textbf{Valid methods.} Among the five methods valid everywhere (Z-STAR, StyTr$^2$, AesPA-Net,
Seedream~4.5, and WEAVE), WEAVE has the highest \dinoc{} on all three benchmarks (0.810/0.861/0.772) and the
lowest LPIPS on D5 and R5. It surpasses the commercial Seedream~4.5 in \dinos{} on D5 (0.492 vs.\ 0.486) at
about half its LPIPS (0.260 vs.\ 0.477). At 512\,px, no method, valid or not, beats WEAVE on a style and a
content metric at once: WEAVE is Pareto-optimal against all twelve baselines in every style--content pair
on both D5 and R5.

\noindent\textbf{Cost.} WEAVE trains 28$\times$ faster than the fastest learned baseline and over
300$\times$ faster than SaMam (1.4 vs.\ 39.5 and 436\,min), runs at 168\,ms per 512\,px image including VAE
decoding, and is 11$\times$ smaller and at least 11$\times$ faster at inference than every other valid
learned model.

\noindent\textbf{Qualitative comparison.} Fig.~\ref{fig:qual} shows both failure modes on an extreme request,
and the close-ups show what the metrics measure: WEAVE keeps the geometry of the source and rewrites its
texture and palette.

\subsection{Ablations}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} removes one component at a time on the same 750 requests; inference-time
controls reuse the selected checkpoint, and retrained variants (seed 42) are reported at their best-\dinos{}
epoch, which favors them. Every component we keep pays for itself (seed s.d.\ of \dinos{}: 0.003). Without
stepwise AdaIN the output slides back toward the source (\dinos{} $-$0.010, \clips{} $-$0.012). Without
texture conditioning \dinos{} drops by 0.009 and LPIPS rises by 0.024. Replacing the anchored endpoint by
the direct target costs the most content (LPIPS $+$0.054, \dinoc{} $-$0.014), exactly as
Proposition~\ref{prop:anchor} predicts, and delays the style peak by nine epochs: the anchor is what makes
82-second training possible. Raising $\lambda_{LL}$ to 1.0 leaves \dinos{} unchanged but costs 0.023
\dinoc{}, and a learned HH head adds content cost (LPIPS $+$0.008) for a marginal style gain, so WEAVE
omits it.

\subsection{Robustness and Transfer}
\noindent\textbf{Seeds and stopping.} Without any image metric in the loop, the stopping rule picks epochs
4, 4, and 3 for seeds 42, 7, and 123 (\dinos{} $0.490\pm0.003$, LPIPS $0.261\pm0.006$) and hits the
\dinos{} peak exactly on both seeds with full 15-epoch curves.

\noindent\textbf{Reference pools.} Redrawing the \dinos{} reference pool 1{,}000 times with 8 of the 30
references per style keeps the margin over IDT positive in every draw (0.030, 95\% interval
$[0.020,0.039]$), essentially equal to the full-pool margin (0.033): the gain does not hinge on any
particular reference, including the one WEAVE conditions on.

\noindent\textbf{Unseen families.} The D5 checkpoint transfers zero-shot to five held-out WikiArt families
with the best \clips{}, LPIPS, and \dinoc{} (Table~\ref{tab:transfer}, top); SaMam's 0.015 \dinos{} edge
costs it 0.12 LPIPS.

\noindent\textbf{Plug-in for a frozen editor.} The same Haar-domain alignment (LH/HL/HH at scale 0.8, LL
untouched) upgrades a frozen SD1.5 image-to-image pipeline (empty prompt, strength 0.5, 20 DDIM steps)
without retraining: on 600 cross-style D5 pairs, \dinos{} and \clips{} rise significantly (paired $t$-test,
$p<10^{-15}$) for $+$0.004 LPIPS and 4.9\,ms per image (Table~\ref{tab:transfer}, bottom).

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Standard metrics reward copying, and two zero-cost references are enough to catch it: under them, eight
of twelve baselines fail, and every compact one falls below the identity in style on some benchmark. The
cause is spectral, and WEAVE removes it at the source: it anchors structure, learns texture transport in
explicit subbands, and injects high-frequency statistics along the trajectory. The result is the only
compact model that is valid everywhere and Pareto-optimal at 512\,px, trained in 1.4 minutes with 1.04M
