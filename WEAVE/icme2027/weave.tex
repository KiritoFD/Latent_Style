\begin{abstract}
Lightweight style transfer suits consumer devices, yet compact models often return images whose
style is no closer to the target than the unchanged input, and content metrics reward this identity
shortcut. We expose it with two trivial references, the source and a reference image of the
target style, which bracket valid transfer; on three benchmarks, eight of thirteen compared methods
leave this bracket. We trace the shortcut to the training signal: in latent velocity matching,
coarse structure carries most of the gradient energy but is the least style-discriminative band.
WEAVE resolves this in the Haar subbands of a frozen autoencoder latent. A source-anchored target
provably shrinks the structural motion, a compact network learns band-weighted texture transport,
and stepwise statistical alignment injects high-frequency style. With 1.04M trainable parameters
and 1.4 minutes of training on one consumer GPU, WEAVE stays valid on all benchmarks and
preserves the most content among valid methods.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Style transfer has moved from research demos into camera apps, creative tools, and on-device media
pipelines, where memory and latency are tight. The strongest results come from diffusion editors and
image foundation models that run a billion-parameter denoiser per image, some needing more than
60\,GB of GPU memory~\cite{wu2025qwenimagetechnicalreport,blackforestlabs2025flux2}. Compact models
fit these budgets~\cite{wang2023microast,liu2024samst,liu2024samam}. This paper asks whether their
outputs actually change style, and how to build a compact model that does.

\noindent\textbf{The identity shortcut.} Copying the input already reaches a \clips{} of 0.693 on our
main benchmark, higher than two recent compact models (0.582 and 0.618). It is also ranked first by
ArtFID~\cite{wright2022artfid} (Fig.~\ref{fig:page1}c) and is optimal for LPIPS~\cite{zhang2018unreasonable}.
We therefore report two trivial outputs with every metric: IDT returns the source and TGT returns a
reference image of the target style. A valid transfer must beat IDT on style and stay closer to the
source than TGT on content. Under this IDT--TGT sandwich, eight of thirteen compared methods fail on
at least one benchmark (Fig.~\ref{fig:page1}b): some are no more stylized than a copy, others discard
as much content as the reference itself.

\noindent\textbf{Why compact models take it.} Style transfer must keep structure while changing
texture, yet a velocity field regressed on the whole latent scores both with one loss. In Haar
coordinates this loss is dominated by the coarse LL band, which carries 69.5\% of the gradient energy
across 5{,}000 cross-style pairs while being the least style-discriminative band: its between-/within-style
variance ratio is 0.12, against 0.56 for the finest band HH (Fig.~\ref{fig:spectral}). The style signal
is a small part of the update, and networks that fit low frequencies first~\cite{rahaman2019spectral,xu2022frequency}
easily ignore it. Large editors escape by overwriting the image; compact models settle near the source.
Both failures stem from the same entanglement, which extra width does not remove.

\noindent\textbf{WEAVE} disentangles the two goals by assigning them to subbands of the latent of a
frozen SD1.5 VAE~\cite{rombach2022high}. A source-anchored endpoint restricts the requested LL motion to
a channel-wise affine change of the source, which we prove removes at least 91\% of its energy and cuts
LL's share from 69.5\% to at most 17\%. A 1.04M-parameter velocity network learns band-weighted transport
whose texture heads are steered by pooled, coordinate-free codes of the reference, and an AdaIN update
aligns high-frequency statistics after every Euler step. Training takes 82.8\,s on one RTX~3060 and stops
at an internal gradient transition without image evaluation. WEAVE is the only compared model under 10M
trainable parameters that is valid on all three benchmarks; among the five valid methods, it preserves
the most content on every benchmark with 11--34$\times$ fewer trainable parameters than the other learned ones.

\noindent\textbf{Contributions.}
(1)~\emph{Evaluation:} the IDT--TGT sandwich, a metric-agnostic check in raw units that exposes the
identity shortcut and content collapse, with an audit of thirteen methods on three benchmarks.
(2)~\emph{Analysis:} a subband measurement and a provable bound that link low-frequency gradient
dominance to the design of the transport target.
(3)~\emph{Method:} wavelet-decoupled velocity matching with orientation-specific texture conditioning,
stepwise high-frequency alignment, and metric-free stopping.
(4)~\emph{Evidence:} the best content preservation among valid methods at 1.04M parameters and
1.4 training minutes, with matched ablations, three seeds, reference-pool resampling, zero-shot transfer,
and a training-free plug-in for a frozen SD1.5 editor.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\noindent\textbf{Arbitrary and lightweight style transfer.} Gatys et al.\ optimized Gram
statistics~\cite{gatys2015neural,gatys2016image}; AdaIN and WCT made transfer
feed-forward~\cite{huang2017arbitrary,li2017universal}. SANet, StyTr$^2$, and AesPA-Net add
attention~\cite{park2019sanet,deng2022stytr2,hong2023aespa}, ArtFlow avoids content leak with reversible
flows~\cite{an2021artflow}, and CUT learns unpaired translation by patch contrast~\cite{park2020cut}.
MicroAST uses micro encoders and a micro decoder~\cite{wang2023microast}, and Mamba-based SaMST and SaMam
reduce sequence cost~\cite{liu2024samst,liu2024samam,gu2023mamba}. Frequency cues appear in FDD, which
mixes content and style spectra~\cite{li2023fdd}, in AesFA, which decomposes features by
frequency~\cite{kwon2024aesfa}, and in the wavelet-corrected WCT$^2$~\cite{yoo2019photorealistic}. These
methods separate frequencies inside a feed-forward decoder; WEAVE uses Haar subbands as the coordinates
of a transport objective with per-band targets, weights, and updates, derived from a measured imbalance.

\noindent\textbf{Diffusion editors and fast translation.} Latent diffusion~\cite{rombach2022high} enables
SDEdit, Prompt-to-Prompt, StyleID, StyleAligned, InST, Z-STAR, IP-Adapter, and
StyleShot~\cite{meng2021sdedit,hertz2022prompt,chung2024style,hertz2024style,zhang2023inst,deng2024zstar,ye2023ipadapter,gao2024styleshot}.
SD-Turbo distills sampling to one step~\cite{sauer2023adversarial}, and LBM performs one-step latent
bridge matching~\cite{chadebec2025lbm}. All of them run a large backbone per image; WEAVE keeps only the
frozen VAE, and our plug-in study tests its Haar operator inside such an editor.

\noindent\textbf{Flow matching and frequency-aware generation.} Rectified flow and flow
matching~\cite{liu2022rectified,lipman2022flow} regress velocities along endpoint interpolations, and
Schr\"odinger bridges~\cite{debortoli2021dsb,shi2023dsbm,liu2023i2sb,su2023ddib} handle unpaired domains.
FDIT constrains frequency bands separately for translation~\cite{cai2021fdit}, FreqFlow reweights
frequencies for class-conditional generation~\cite{ren2026freqflow}, and wavelet diffusion accelerates
sampling~\cite{phung2023wavelet}. WEAVE changes the endpoint, basis, and band weights of the regression itself.

\noindent\textbf{Evaluation.} LPIPS~\cite{zhang2018unreasonable} and ArtFID~\cite{wright2022artfid} are
standard, and a recent review finds no consensus on style-transfer evaluation~\cite{ioannou2024evaluation}.
The sandwich adds two zero-cost references that give each metric an explicit direction.

\section{Diagnosing the Identity Shortcut}
\label{sec:diagnosis}

\subsection{The IDT--TGT Sandwich}
A request consists of a source image $x$ and a target style $s$, represented by a reference image $r_s$.
We set $y_{\mathrm{IDT}}=x$ and $y_{\mathrm{TGT}}=r_s$ and call a method \emph{valid} on a benchmark if
its benchmark means satisfy
\begin{equation}
S(y)>S(y_{\mathrm{IDT}})\ \ \forall S\in\mathcal S,\qquad
C(y)\succ C(y_{\mathrm{TGT}})\ \ \forall C\in\mathcal C,
\label{eq:sandwich}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal S=\{\text{\dinos},\text{\clips}\}$, $\mathcal C=\{\text{LPIPS},\text{\dinoc}\}$, and $\succ$
means closer to the source. The first condition excludes the identity shortcut and the second excludes
content collapse. Both references are free to compute, keep raw metric units, and apply to any metric pair;
we check each inequality separately instead of forming a weighted score. They are empirical anchors, not
bounds: a failure flags a regime for visual inspection on that benchmark. Copying is a strong baseline
because content images already share features with any art collection: on D5, IDT scores \dinos{}~0.419
and \clips{}~0.693.

\subsection{Spectral Dominance in Latent Velocity Matching}
Let $\mathcal W(z)=(\ell,h_1,h_2,h_3)$ be the one-level orthonormal Haar transform of a latent
$z\in\mathbb R^{4\times H\times W}$, with LL band $\ell$ and the oriented bands LH, HL, and HH. Flow
matching~\cite{lipman2022flow,liu2022rectified} regresses a velocity $v$ onto the displacement $u=z_1-z_0$
between endpoints. By Parseval, $\|v-u\|^2=\sum_b\|v_b-u_b\|^2$: the loss splits into independent band terms,
and at initialization the share of band $b$ in the gradient energy equals its share of the displacement
energy, $\|u_b\|^2/\|u\|^2$. Fig.~\ref{fig:spectral} measures both on 5{,}000 cross-style D5 pairs with the
direct target $z_1=z_s$. LL holds 69.5\% of the gradient energy, yet its style separability, the
between-/within-style variance ratio of per-image band statistics, is the lowest (0.12 versus 0.56 for HH).
The objective is driven by the band that matters least for style, and moving $\ell_c$ toward $\ell_s$ asks
the network to reproduce the unrelated layout of $x_s$, which it can neither predict nor should copy.


\section{WEAVE}
\label{sec:method}


\subsection{Setting}
WEAVE is trained on unpaired image collections of $K$ styles. A request names a target style by its index
$s$ and one reference image $x_s$; at test time this is the first held-out image of the style, the same
image used for TGT. We work on latents $z_c=\mathcal E(x_c)$ and $z_s=\mathcal E(x_s)$ of the frozen VAE,
cached once before training, and write $P_Lz=\mathcal W^{-1}(\ell,0,0,0)$ and $P_H=I-P_L$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:framework}).

\subsection{Source-Anchored Endpoint}
WEAVE anchors structure at the source and takes texture from the reference:
\begin{equation}
z_1^\star=\mathcal W^{-1}\big((1{-}\alpha)\ell_c+\alpha\,\mathrm{AdaIN}(\ell_c,\ell_s),\;h_{1,s},h_{2,s},h_{3,s}\big),
\label{eq:target}
\end{equation}
with $\alpha=0.3$ and channel-wise spatial statistics in AdaIN~\cite{huang2017arbitrary}.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:anchor}
For one channel, let $\ell_c,\ell_s\in\mathbb R^N$ have means $\mu_c,\mu_s$, standard deviations
$\sigma_c,\sigma_s$, and correlation $\rho$. The LL velocity requested by \eqref{eq:target},
$u_\ell=\alpha(\mathrm{AdaIN}(\ell_c,\ell_s)-\ell_c)$, is an affine function of $\ell_c$ and
\begin{equation}
\|u_\ell\|^2=\alpha^2N\big[(\sigma_s-\sigma_c)^2+(\mu_s-\mu_c)^2\big]\le\alpha^2\|\ell_s-\ell_c\|^2.
\label{eq:bound}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{IEEEproof}
$\mathrm{AdaIN}(\ell_c,\ell_s)-\ell_c=(\sigma_s/\sigma_c-1)(\ell_c-\mu_c)+(\mu_s-\mu_c)$ gives the equality.
Since $\|\ell_s-\ell_c\|^2=N[\sigma_s^2+\sigma_c^2-2\rho\sigma_s\sigma_c+(\mu_s-\mu_c)^2]$, the gap is
$2N\sigma_s\sigma_c(1-\rho)\ge0$.
\end{IEEEproof}

Two consequences follow. The requested structural motion only changes per-channel gain and offset,
i.e., palette and contrast, and contains no layout of $x_s$. Because the high-frequency target is
unchanged, averaging \eqref{eq:bound} over the pairs of Fig.~\ref{fig:spectral} lowers LL's share of
the displacement energy from 69.5\% to at most 17.0\%. Training samples
$z_t=(1-t)z_c+tz_1^\star-\sigma\sqrt{t(1-t)}\,\epsilon$ with $t\sim\mathcal U[0,1]$,
$\epsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,I)$, and $\sigma=0.02$; the label is the clean displacement $u=z_1^\star-z_c$,
and the perturbation, which vanishes at both endpoints, is absent at inference.

\subsection{Band-Weighted Velocity Matching}
The network $v_\theta(z_t,t,s,x_s)$ has a shared trunk of four width-64 residual blocks and separate
$1{\times}1$ heads for LL, LH, and HL. With $(u_\ell,u_{h_1},u_{h_2},u_{h_3})=\mathcal W(u)$, it minimizes
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L=\mathbb E_{t}\big[\lambda_{LL}\|v_\ell-u_\ell\|^2+\|v_{h_1}-u_{h_1}\|^2+\|v_{h_2}-u_{h_2}\|^2\big],
\label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
with $\lambda_{LL}=0.3$. The endpoint \eqref{eq:target} changes the \emph{requested} LL velocity, whereas
$\lambda_{LL}$ changes its optimization pressure. A style memory indexed by $s$ gives the trunk a coarse
prior. For image-specific texture, the reference LH and HL bands are pooled into coordinate-free,
orientation-specific codes that modulate only the matching heads; feeding raw spatial reference bands
instead leaks the reference layout (\dinoc{} falls to 0.40 in our probes; see supplement). HH has no
learned head. The model has 1{,}037{,}087 trainable parameters.

\subsection{Stepwise High-Frequency Alignment}
Velocity matching organizes the motion but leaves a weak appearance signal. With $q=P_Hz$, $q_s=P_Hz_s$,
and $\mathcal A(q;q_s)=\sigma(q_s)(q-\mu(q))/\sigma(q)+\mu(q_s)$ over channel-wise spatial statistics, each
of the $K$ inference steps is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\bar z^{k+1}&=z^k+\tfrac1K\,\mathcal W^{-1}\big(v_\ell^k,v_{h_1}^k,v_{h_2}^k,0\big),\\
z^{k+1}&=P_L\bar z^{k+1}+(1-\beta)\,\bar q+\beta\,\mathcal A\big(\bar q;(1+\eta)q_s\big),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:step}
\end{equation}
where $\bar q=P_H\bar z^{k+1}$, $K=8$, $\beta=2$, and $\eta=0.1$. With $\beta>1$ the update over-relaxes
toward the matched statistics; applied at every step, alignment becomes part of the trajectory rather than
a post-process and supplies HH statistics without a learned head.

\subsection{Metric-Free Stopping}
Training needs no decoding, inversion, or discriminator and runs 52 steps per epoch at batch 96. After
each epoch, a probe batch of four fixed latents at $t=0.5$ gives $\rho_e$, the ratio of LL-loss to HF-loss
gradient norms on the shared trunk. Training stops at the first epoch in which the gate of the texture
route grows and $\rho_e/\rho_{e-1}\le0.65$, i.e., when high-frequency learning takes over the trunk. On D5
the rule fires at epoch~4 ($\rho$ drops from 1.09 to 0.16) after 82.8\,s, without any image metric.

\subsection{Setup}
\noindent\textbf{Benchmarks.} \emph{D5-512}: five WikiArt styles (Early Renaissance, Impressionism,
Minimalism, Rococo, Ukiyo-e) at 512\,px with 3{,}600 training images each; 30 held-out sources per style are
sent to all five styles (750 requests, same-style requests kept). \emph{P2A-256}: the same layout over photo,
Monet, Van Gogh, C\'ezanne, and Hayao at 256\,px. \emph{R5-WikiArt}: five randomly drawn WikiArt families
(Cubism, Expressionism, Pop Art, Romanticism, Symbolism) at 512\,px.

\noindent\textbf{Metrics.} \dinos{} (primary) is the maximum cosine similarity between the DINOv2-small CLS
feature~\cite{oquab2023dinov2} of the output and those of up to 30 held-out references of the target style;
it rewards resembling some member of the family rather than its average. \clips{} is the CLIP
ViT-B/32~\cite{radford2021clip} similarity to the target-style prototype, \dinoc{} the DINOv2 similarity to
the source, and LPIPS uses AlexNet.

\noindent\textbf{Baselines and implementation.} We compare training-free diffusion editors
(SD-Turbo~\cite{sauer2023adversarial}, StyleAligned, Z-STAR, StyleShot, StyleID), learned models (CUT, SaMST,
SaMam, StyTr$^2$, AesPA-Net), the Seedream~4.5 API~\cite{seedream2025}, and an analytic Latent-WCT, each at its
released or recommended operating point. WEAVE uses AdamW (learning rate $2\times10^{-4}$, cosine decay),
bf16, and one RTX~3060 (12\,GB). Training time excludes one-off latent caching; inference time includes
eight ODE steps and VAE decoding.


\subsection{Main Results}
\noindent\textbf{The audit.} Only five of thirteen methods are valid on all three benchmarks
(Table~\ref{tab:main}, Fig.~\ref{fig:page1}b), and the failures are instructive. SD-Turbo attains the best
D5 LPIPS (0.003) because it barely edits the input: its \clips{} equals that of IDT. SaMam, the strongest
compact baseline on content, has \clips{} below IDT on two benchmarks, so its low LPIPS partly reflects the
identity shortcut. StyleAligned has the highest \dinos{} everywhere but moves farther from the source than
the reference itself on D5 and R5. Latent-WCT never clears IDT: wavelet statistics alone do not transfer
style, and the learned transport is necessary.

\noindent\textbf{Valid methods.} Among the five valid methods (Z-STAR, StyTr$^2$, AesPA-Net, Seedream~4.5,
and WEAVE), WEAVE has the highest \dinoc{} on all three benchmarks (0.810/0.861/0.772) and the lowest LPIPS
on D5 and R5. Its \dinos{} lies within the range of the others on D5 and R5 and is 0.018 lower on P2A, where
each Haar subband of the 256\,px latent holds only $16{\times}16$ coefficients per channel. On D5, WEAVE
matches the commercial Seedream~4.5 in \dinos{} (0.492 vs.\ 0.486) at about half its LPIPS (0.260 vs.\ 0.477).
Among all thirteen methods, no competitor beats WEAVE on a style and a content metric at once on D5 and R5,
i.e., WEAVE is Pareto-optimal in all four style--content metric pairs there. On P2A, SaMam is better on all
four metrics, albeit with 8.5$\times$ the parameters and over 300$\times$ the training time, and Seedream is
better on style and LPIPS.

\noindent\textbf{Cost.} WEAVE has the fewest trainable parameters and the shortest training among the
learned methods we compare (1.4\,min vs.\ 39.5--1440\,min) and needs 168\,ms per 512\,px image including VAE
decoding. Among valid methods, the next cheapest learned model (AesPA-Net) has 11$\times$ more trainable
parameters and 11$\times$ longer inference.

\noindent\textbf{Qualitative comparison.} Fig.~\ref{fig:qual} shows both failure modes on an extreme
request. The close-ups show what the metrics measure: WEAVE keeps the geometry of the source and changes
its texture and palette.


\subsection{Ablations}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} changes one component at a time on the same 750 requests. Inference-time controls
reuse the selected checkpoint; retrained variants (seed 42) are reported at their best-\dinos{} epoch, which
favors them. As a yardstick, \dinos{} varies by 0.003 (s.d.) across seeds. Removing stepwise AdaIN moves the
output toward the source (\dinos{} $-$0.010, \clips{} $-$0.012, LPIPS $-$0.034). Removing the texture
conditioning lowers \dinos{} by 0.009 and raises LPIPS by 0.024 while \clips{} is nearly unchanged. Replacing
the anchored endpoint by the direct target has the largest content cost (LPIPS $+$0.054, \dinoc{} $-$0.014), as
Proposition~\ref{prop:anchor} predicts, and its style peak arrives nine epochs later. Raising $\lambda_{LL}$
to 1.0 changes peak \dinos{} by less than one seed s.d.\ but lowers \dinoc{} by 0.023, and a learned HH head
gives a within-noise style gain at a small content cost, so we keep the simpler model.

\subsection{Robustness and Transfer}
\noindent\textbf{Seeds and stopping.} The stopping rule selects epochs 4, 4, and 3 for seeds 42, 7, and 123
(\dinos{} $0.490\pm0.003$, LPIPS $0.261\pm0.006$); for the two seeds with full 15-epoch curves, the selected
epoch is the \dinos{} peak.

\noindent\textbf{Reference pools.} WEAVE conditions on one reference per style that also belongs to the
\dinos{} pool. We therefore redraw the pool: with 8 of the 30 references per style, sampled 1{,}000 times so
that the conditioning image is usually absent, the mean \dinos{} margin over IDT stays positive in every draw
(0.030, 95\% interval $[0.020, 0.039]$) and close to the full-pool margin (0.033); about 69\% of individual
requests improve.

\noindent\textbf{Unseen families.} Applied without retraining to five held-out WikiArt families
(Table~\ref{tab:transfer}, top), the D5 checkpoint gives the best \clips{}, LPIPS, and \dinoc{}; SaMam is
0.015 higher in \dinos{} at a much higher LPIPS.

\noindent\textbf{Plug-in for a frozen editor.} Adding the same Haar-domain alignment (LH/HL/HH at scale 0.8,
LL untouched) to a frozen SD1.5 image-to-image pipeline (empty prompt, strength 0.5, 20 DDIM steps) raises
\dinos{} and \clips{} on 600 cross-style D5 pairs (paired $t$-test, $p<10^{-15}$) at a small LPIPS change
(Table~\ref{tab:transfer}, bottom), for 4.9\,ms per image and no weight update.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\noindent\textbf{Limitations.} WEAVE uses one SD1.5 VAE and a single Haar level; styles defined by global
layout or spanning several scales may need deeper decompositions, and at 256\,px the subbands are coarse.
Haar separates latent coordinates, not semantic factors, and the nonlinear decoder can couple them. The
stopping rule is validated on one architecture and three seeds. IDT and TGT are benchmark-specific anchors
measured with imperfect perceptual proxies, and baselines run at their recommended operating points rather
than under a matched compute budget.

\noindent\textbf{Conclusion.} The identity shortcut hides behind metrics that reward it. Two trivial
references make it visible, and under them most compared methods fail somewhere. Its cause is spectral,
since coarse structure dominates the gradient but carries little style, and WEAVE removes it by anchoring
structure at the source, learning texture transport in explicit subbands, and injecting high-frequency
statistics along the trajectory. The result is the most content-preserving valid transfer we measured, from
a 1.04M-parameter model trained in 1.4 minutes, which makes faithful style transfer practical on consumer
hardware. Code, checkpoints, and the 750-request protocol will be released.
